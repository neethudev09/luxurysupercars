### The Documents: Simpler Than You Think

This is where most guides overcomplicate things. Here's the truth:

If your driving licence was issued in **the US, UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, or most of the EU**, you can rent a supercar in Dubai using your home licence *directly*. No International Driving Permit needed. The [RTA exempts these countries](/faq) under a reciprocal agreement.

If your licence is from **India, China, Russia, Brazil, South Africa, or most of Asia/Africa/South America**, you need an International Driving Permit — arranged *before* you travel. You cannot get one in Dubai. This is the single most common reason tourists get turned away at handover, and it's entirely avoidable.

What you'll need at pickup:
- Your **passport** (original, with UAE entry stamp)
- Your **home driving licence** (physical, not a photo)
- An **International Driving Permit** (only if your country isn't exempt)
- A **credit card** in your name (for the security deposit)

That's it. No special test. No supercar licence. No office visit required.

### The Deposit Question: Why It's Not the Problem Reddit Says It Is

If you've spent any time on Reddit, you've seen the horror stories. AED 10,000 holds that take months to release. Companies that vanish with deposits. Cash demands at pickup.

Here's the distinction that matters: **reputable companies vs. brokers.**

A legitimate operator with a physical showroom, verified Google reviews, and transparent terms *will return your deposit*. The hold is a pre-authorisation on your credit card, not a charge. At [Luxury Supercars Dubai](/about-us), deposits are processed through proper channels and released once Salik tolls and any post-rental fines have cleared — typically within 15 working days.

What separates a trustworthy company from a risky one:

The deposit amount varies by car. A Lamborghini Huracán typically requires AED 5,000–10,000. A Ferrari F8 is similar. A Rolls-Royce Cullinan sits at the higher end. But here's the key: *if a company is transparent about this upfront, that's a green flag, not a red one.*

## The Routes That Make It All Worthwhile

You've got the car. Now where do you drive it?

**Sheikh Zayed Road at dusk.** The canyon of towers lighting up as the sun drops behind the desert. This is the defining Dubai supercar moment — windows down, exhaust in Sport mode, the Burj Khalifa growing in your windscreen.

**Palm Jumeirah crescent.** The approach to Atlantis The Royal is the most photographed supercar backdrop in the city for a reason. Time it for sunset and you'll understand why.

**Dubai Marina to JBR.** Coastal cruising at its best. Pull up at one of the beachfront restaurants, hand the keys to valet, and watch the evening unfold.

**Jebel Jais (Ras Al Khaimah).** Ninety minutes from Dubai, the UAE's highest mountain road — 22 km of smooth tarmac climbing to 1,700 metres. Go early morning for the most atmospheric drive in the country. [Book a Range Rover or G63](/our-fleet) for this trip — the low-clearance supercars handle it, but the SUVs do it in more comfort.

**Abu Dhabi bound.** The E11 highway is a grand tourer's dream. Visit the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque, drive the Yas Marina Circuit, or have lunch at the Etihad Towers. The 280 km round trip fits comfortably within a standard 250 km daily mileage cap.*
